## fig1_design

Study design. Each tool profile (five technical inputs) is simulated in a stochastic common-bean breeding program; its genetic gain over the check varieties is calibrated to realized progress, valued through variety adoption and discounting over the economic states, and attributed to its inputs. The colors identify the model inputs in all later figures.

## fig2_time_beats_accuracy

In the simulated pipeline, cycle time outweighs accuracy. (a) Annual genetic-gain uplift over manual phenotyping across all simulated tool profiles, by years of cycle compression, which is applied as a scenario and not simulated (cloud: density; rain: profiles; bar and ring: interquartile range and median). Orange: evidence-based profile (error reduction 10%, cost reduction 20%). (b) Exchange rate between accuracy and cycle time: years of breeding-cycle compression that raise annual genetic gain as much as the tool's per-cycle uplift, at the evidence-based (10%) and an optimistic (50%) error reduction, by plot-level heritability (large markers: mean over the GxE-to-genetic variance ratio κ = 0.4-3; small dots: individual settings).

## fig3_gain_ceiling

Accuracy and cost savings move per-cycle genetic gain by a few percent. (a, b) Per-cycle genetic-gain uplift of the tool over manual phenotyping across all simulated tool profiles, by measurement-error reduction (a) and whole-plot cost reduction (b) (h2 = 0.10; cloud: density; rain: profiles; bar and ring: interquartile range and median); shaded columns: evidence-based values. (c) Robustness: per-cycle uplift with no and with the largest error reduction for each combination of plot-level heritability and GxE-to-genetic variance ratio κ. Blue lines: the annual-gain increase from one year of cycle compression. (d) Robustness to a fixed cost per environment and stage, paid by both programs and not reduced by the tool: per-cycle uplift per 10-point error reduction and per 10-point cost reduction (least-squares slopes over the tool profiles of the block, with 95% intervals), at h2 = 0.10 and κ = 1.5.

## fig4_value_follows_time

Value follows cycle time. (a) NPV of the tool across all tool profiles and economic states by cycle compression (violins of positive values on a log scale; labels, bar and ring: median and interquartile range of all states; orange diamonds: evidence-based profile). (b) Median equivalent annual value per hectare by cycle compression and bean price (discount rate 8%, adoption ceiling 30%). (c) Sobol total-order indices by evaluation horizon; the four inputs with the largest index at the final year are labelled. (d) Median and mean NPV in the pessimistic and optimistic corners of the input space and in the remaining (mixed) states; P(loss): share of states with negative NPV.

## fig5_what_to_measure

What a pilot should measure. (a) First-order (open) and total-order (filled) Sobol indices of NPV. (b) Importance ranks of the inputs under five measures: Sobol total-order index, Shapley effects with independent and copula-correlated inputs, PAWN median and EVPPI at the indifference hurdle; the four minor technical inputs (dots in the shaded band, colors as in panel a) lie below every other input on every measure, and their order is not resolved. (c) Expected value of perfect partial information of each input as the payoff of the alternative investment varies (multiple of expected NPV); labels give the three largest values at the indifference point.

## fig6_evidence

Evidence behind the model inputs. (a) Realized yield gain of common-bean breeding programs (k = 8 estimates, 4 programs, I2 = 87%; diamond and band: three-level REML estimate and 95% CI). (b) Agreement between image-based or AI tools and manual reference measurements by trait (k = 19 effects; line and band: pooled estimate and 95% CI). (c) Time saved by digital phenotyping relative to manual recording, by platform (line: pooled estimate; two descriptive units for handheld tools). (d) Ranges of measurement-error and whole-plot cost reduction explored by the model (bars; dots: central case) against the evidence (points: median or estimate; lines: 95% interval, or range for heritability pairs; arrows: interval continues beyond -115%).

## fig7_cycle_pathway

How phenotyping efficiency reaches genetic gain per year. (a) The four-stage pipeline from cross to release (ON, observation nursery; PYT, preliminary yield trial; AYT, advanced yield trial; NPT, national performance trial), with the assumed cycle length L = 10 years. For each stage, bars mark the waits that can hold back the next planting or crossing decision: season-limited (growing season), data-limited (scoring, image analysis and data cleaning between harvest and the decision; blue, the only wait that faster phenotyping can shorten), seed-limited (seed multiplication) and regulatory (official variety testing). The next stage starts when every wait that applies has ended (dashed lines). Bar widths are schematic: the configuration fixes the cycle length and the number of selfing generations, not the duration of single waits. Stage sizes are the manual program's optimal allocation at the central budget (Table 3). The tool program is shorter by c years (c = 1 in the evidence-based profile, 0 to 3 explored), a scenario input that the simulation does not derive. (b) Causal pathway. Faster scoring and analysis (blue, cycle compression) shorten the cycle only if the next step was waiting for data. More accurate measurement (orange, error reduction) and cheaper plots (green, cost reduction) act on per-cycle gain through the effective heritability, selection accuracy and selection intensity. Annual gain is per-cycle gain divided by cycle length. Percentages: the median per-cycle uplift of the evidence-based profile in the simulation (4.3%) and the annual-gain increase from one year of compression, which follows from L by construction (11.1%).
