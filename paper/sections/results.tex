\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

All results are simulated, ex ante and conditional on the explored ranges of the inputs. Per-cycle gains are measured over the check varieties unless stated otherwise. Cycle compression is an assumed scenario input that is applied in closed form and not simulated.

%==============================================================================
\subsection{Public trial data place the program at low heritability and strong GxE}\label{sec:res_params}

The public East African trials give a within-environment plot heritability ($h^2_w$) near \NumEmpTZHtwoAll and a GxE-to-genetic variance ratio ($\kappa$) near \NumEmpTZKappaAll (Table~\ref{tab:params}). Pooled over the \NumEmpTZNenvAll trials of the TSEZVG panel, $h^2_w$ was \NumEmpTZHtwoBush for bush and \NumEmpTZHtwoClimb for climbing beans, and $\kappa$ was \NumEmpTZKappaBush and \NumEmpTZKappaClimb. The connected trial sets and the ZUDPEP series spread these values widely. The variance components published for regional trials \citep{Amongi2023Gain,Amongi2025Yield} give $h^2_w$ of \NumPubHtwoWMin to \NumPubHtwoWMax and $\kappa$ of \NumPubKappaMin to \NumPubKappaMax. The model therefore uses $h^2_w = \NumCfgHtwoYield$ and $\kappa = \NumCfgKappa$ at its center, and its robustness block covers most of the range in the data. Heritability is lower and GxE larger than the values that breeding simulations commonly assume. Low $h^2_w$ makes plot error the largest variance component of a single plot, which a more accurate measurement reduces. High $\kappa$ makes environments, not replicates, the scarce resource, which cheaper plots buy.

The definitions differ, and only like quantities are compared in Table~\ref{tab:params}. Single-trial heritabilities (medians of \NumEmpTrialHtwoMedZD to \NumEmpTrialHtwoMedTZ over \NumEmpNtrials trials) include the GxE variance of that trial and so exceed $h^2_w$. The heritability with GxE in the denominator, $h^2_{\mathrm{total}}$, was \NumPubHtwoPlotMin to \NumPubHtwoPlotMax in the published components. At the model center, the same ratio is \NumChainHtwoONMan, the heritability of a single plot in a single environment. Counting the spatial terms of the published analyses as error lowers their smallest $h^2_w$ to \NumPubHtwoWSpatialMin, closer to the trial data. The model's $h^2_w$ thus matches the pooled trial data and lies just below the published range. Its $\kappa$ lies within both. Methods and estimates by dataset are in Supplementary Section S3.

The data also bound a realistic error reduction. Adjusting plot yield for harvested stand, centered within genotype, cut the plot error variance by a median fraction of \NumStandDeltaEMed (interquartile range \NumStandDeltaELo to \NumStandDeltaEHi) over \NumStandNtrials ZUDPEP trials, close to the evidence-based error reduction of \NumCfgErrRedCentral\%. \subsection{From measurement error to selection decisions}\label{sec:res_chain}

Lower measurement error raised selection accuracy most at the observation nursery, where each line is scored in one plot, and little at the replicated stages (Table~\ref{tab:chain}). At the central $h^2_w$, the evidence-based error reduction raised $h^2_w$ to \NumChainHtwoPlotErrTen and the largest explored reduction (\NumCfgErrRedMax\%) to \NumChainHtwoPlotErrFifty. In the observation nursery, accuracy rose from \NumChainAccONMan to \NumChainAccONErrTen and \NumChainAccONErrFifty, gains of \NumChainAccRelONErrTen\% and \NumChainAccRelONErrFifty\%. In the PYT, AYT and NPT, replication across environments had already raised $h^2_{\mathrm{eff}}$ to between \NumChainHtwoAYTMan and \NumChainHtwoNPTMan, and even the largest reduction raised accuracy by at most \NumChainAccRelAYTErrFifty\%. Error reduction acts only on the plot-error term of Equation~(\ref{eq:heff}), which replication already divides, whereas the GxE term is unchanged. At the lowest explored heritability (\NumCfgRobHtwoLow), the largest reduction raised accuracy by \NumChainLowAccRelONErrFifty\% in the observation nursery and \NumChainLowAccRelPYTErrFifty\% in the PYT, so the replicated stages also gain from accuracy when plot error dominates.

Selection decisions changed in the same pattern. In the observation nursery, \NumChainHitONMan\% of the lines selected under manual phenotyping belonged to the true top \NumChainONSelPct\%. The two error reductions raised this hit rate by \NumChainHitGainONErrTen and \NumChainHitGainONErrFifty percentage points. In the PYT, which selects most intensely, the gains were \NumChainHitGainPYTErrTen and \NumChainHitGainPYTErrFifty points. In the AYT and NPT, the largest reduction added at most \NumChainHitGainAYTErrFifty points. At the lowest heritability, the largest reduction added \NumChainLowHitGainONErrFifty and \NumChainLowHitGainPYTErrFifty points in these two stages.

Summed over the four stages, these decision changes raise per-cycle gain by less than the breeder's equation predicts. Summing the stage responses of the breeder's equation gives uplifts of \NumChainBEUpliftErrTen\% and \NumChainBEUpliftErrFifty\% for the two reductions. At the lowest cost reduction (\NumChainSimCostRed\%), profiles without error reduction changed simulated gain by \NumChainSimUpliftErrZero\% (SE \NumChainSimSEErrZero). Net of this, the two error reductions raised simulated gain by \NumChainSimNetVsBaseErrTen\% (SE \NumChainSimNetVsBaseSEErrTen) and \NumChainSimNetVsBaseErrFifty\% (SE \NumChainSimNetVsBaseSEErrFifty) over the founder mean, the basis of the breeder's equation, and by \NumChainSimNetErrTen\% and \NumChainSimNetErrFifty\% over the check varieties. A normal model of four sequential truncation stages gave \NumChainSeqUpliftErrTen\% and \NumChainSeqUpliftErrFifty\%, close to the simulation. The breeder's-equation sum is an upper bound, because each later stage selects among lines with less genetic variance left.

\subsection{Accuracy and cost levers move per-cycle gain}\label{sec:res_gain}

Per-cycle gain rises steadily with error reduction (Figure~\ref{fig:fig3_gain_ceiling}a). At $h^2_w = \NumCfgHtwoYield$, the median per-cycle uplift over manual phenotyping rose from \NumUpliftErrZero\% at no error reduction to \NumUpliftErrTen\% at the evidence-based value and \NumUpliftErrFifty\% at the largest. One year of compression raises annual gain by \NumCycOneYearPct\%, a level that the median uplift exceeded only at the three largest error reductions (\NumUpliftErrThirty\% at the smallest of them). Over all \NumNcellsOK profiles, the mean paired difference was \NumDGdiffPct\% of the manual gain (\NumDGdiffPerCycle kg/ha per cycle, uncalibrated; Monte Carlo SE \NumDGdiffSE kg/ha). The mean difference was negative in \NumNcellsNegDiff profiles, \NumNcellsNegDiffErrZero of them without error reduction, where the tool's fixed cost displaces plots. The positive uplift at no error reduction measures the cost channel alone.

Cheaper plots raise per-cycle gain because a fixed budget buys more entries and environments (Figure~\ref{fig:fig3_gain_ceiling}b). Raising the cost reduction from \NumCfgCostRedMin\% to \NumCfgCostRedMax\% moved the median uplift from \NumUpliftCostFive\% to \NumUpliftCostSeventy\%. The uplift was larger at the smallest budget (\NumUpliftBudgetLow\%) than at the largest (\NumUpliftBudgetHigh\%), consistent with a budget that limits trial size and with diminishing returns to selection intensity \citep{Cobb2019Enhancing}. At the evidence-based cost reduction of \NumCfgCostRedCentral\%, the median uplift was \NumUpliftCostTwenty\%, of the same order as the median at the evidence-based error reduction (\NumUpliftErrTen\%). Neither median reaches the effect of one year of compression.

Heritability sets the size of the accuracy channel more than GxE does (Figure~\ref{fig:fig3_gain_ceiling}c). Averaged over the $\kappa$ levels, the uplift at no and at the largest error reduction fell with each step up in $h^2_w$, from \NumRobUpliftHtwoFiveErrZero\% and \NumRobUpliftHtwoFiveErrFifty\% at \NumCfgRobHtwoLow through \NumRobUpliftHtwoTenErrZero\% and \NumRobUpliftHtwoTenErrFifty\% at the center to \NumRobUpliftHtwoThirtyErrZero\% and \NumRobUpliftHtwoThirtyErrFifty\% at \NumCfgRobHtwoHigh. Only at the two lowest levels did the largest error reduction raise per-cycle gain beyond the effect of one year of compression.

A fixed cost per environment and stage, paid by both programs, did not dilute either lever (Figure~\ref{fig:fig3_gain_ceiling}d). Over environment costs of USD \NumCfgEnvCostLevels, the uplift per 10-point error reduction was \NumEnvCostZeroErrSlope\% without an environment cost and \NumEnvCostFiveKErrSlope\% at the highest. The uplift per 10-point cost reduction stayed between \NumEnvCostOneKCostSlope\% and \NumEnvCostZeroCostSlope\% at the three lower levels and reached \NumEnvCostFiveKCostSlope\% at the highest. This is consistent with an optimizer that tests in fewer environments when each costs more, which lowers $h^2_{\mathrm{eff}}$ and leaves more for accuracy to recover (Supplementary Section S6).

\subsection{The breeder's equation overstates the accuracy channel}\label{sec:res_be}

The breeder's equation gets the direction and shape of the tool effects right but not their size (Fig.~S3; Supplementary Section S5). Within each heritability level, the correlation between the uplift predicted by the multi-stage breeder's equation, with both programs re-optimized, and the simulated uplift was \NumBEcorMin to \NumBEcorMax. The simulated accuracy effect was only a fraction of the predicted one: \NumBEratioHtwoTen at the central heritability and from \NumBEratioHtwoFive at $h^2_w = \NumCfgRobHtwoLow$ to \NumBEratioHtwoThirty at \NumCfgRobHtwoHigh (founder-mean basis, the larger explored error reductions). Selection at each stage depletes the genetic variance that the next stage selects on, which the breeder's equation ignores. Sizing the accuracy effect with the breeder's equation would thus nearly double it at the central heritability. The uplifts in the other sections are over the check varieties and are larger at low heritability.

The breeder's equation also misses how the cost channel grows with GxE. At the central heritability and a cost reduction of \NumBEcostRedMax\%, the simulated uplift was \NumBEcostKappaLoSim\% at $\kappa = \NumCfgRobKappaLow$ and \NumBEcostKappaHiSim\% at $\kappa = \NumCfgRobKappaHigh$, against \NumBEcostKappaLoBE\% and \NumBEcostKappaHiBE\% from the breeder's equation. Under strong GxE, the plots that a cheaper tool frees buy environments, and the number of environments limits the heritability of a line mean. The breeder's equation, which sees only the change in $h^2_{\mathrm{eff}}$, doubles the cost channel at the lowest $\kappa$ and changes less across $\kappa$ than the simulation.

\subsection{Exchange rate between accuracy and cycle time}\label{sec:res_time}

Years of cycle compression sort the annual-gain uplift of the tool profiles into four groups, and accuracy and cost spread profiles within each group (Figure~\ref{fig:fig2_time_beats_accuracy}a). This separation follows from arithmetic: compression is applied in closed form, as per-cycle gain divided by a shorter cycle, and one year less of a \NumCfgCycleYears-year cycle raises annual gain by \NumCycOneYearPct\%. The median annual uplift was \NumAnnUpliftCycZero\% at no compression and \NumAnnUpliftCycOne\%, \NumAnnUpliftCycTwo\% and \NumAnnUpliftCycThree\% at one to three years. The interquartile ranges of the four groups did not overlap (from \NumAnnUpliftCycZeroQone\% to \NumAnnUpliftCycZeroQthree\% at no compression and from \NumAnnUpliftCycThreeQone\% to \NumAnnUpliftCycThreeQthree\% at three years). The evidence-based profile (error reduction \NumCfgErrRedCentral\%, cost reduction \NumCfgCostRedCentral\%) lay below the group median at every level, with a median per-cycle uplift of \NumUpliftMeta\% and annual uplifts of \NumAnnUpliftMetaCycZero\% to \NumAnnUpliftMetaCycThree\%.

At the heritability of the data, accuracy is worth months of compression, and an optimistic error reduction more than a year (Figure~\ref{fig:fig2_time_beats_accuracy}b). At $h^2_w = \NumCfgHtwoYield$, the per-cycle uplift at the evidence-based error reduction equaled \NumExchMoHtwoTenErrTen months of compression, and that at \NumCfgErrRedMax\% equaled \NumExchMoHtwoTenErrFifty months. The equivalents, which include the cost reductions that the profiles also carry, fell with heritability, from \NumExchMoHtwoFiveErrTen and \NumExchMoHtwoFiveErrFifty months at $h^2_w = \NumCfgRobHtwoLow$ to \NumExchMoHtwoThirtyErrTen and \NumExchMoHtwoThirtyErrFifty months at \NumCfgRobHtwoHigh, a value closer to those often assumed. The breeder's equation already implies that accuracy matters more at low heritability. The simulation adds the size of the exchange rate at the heritability of the data, where an optimistic error reduction is worth more than one year of a \NumCfgCycleYears-year cycle.

\subsection{What a pilot should measure}\label{sec:res_measure}

Cycle compression had the largest share of NPV variance, with discount rate and price close behind and adoption ceiling next (Figure~\ref{fig:fig5_what_to_measure}a). The total-order Sobol indices \citep{Pianosi2016Sensitivity} were \NumSobolSTCyc for compression, \NumSobolSTDisc for discount rate, \NumSobolSTPrice for price and \NumSobolSTAdopt for adoption ceiling. Error reduction and cost reduction had indices of \NumSobolSTErrRed and \NumSobolSTCostRed, and program budget and tool fixed cost \NumSobolSTBudget and \NumSobolSTFixCost. Adding Layer~1 simulation noise moved no total-order index by more than \NumSobolBootMaxShift. The first-order indices summed to \NumSobolSumSone, so interactions, expected for an NPV that multiplies gain, price, adoption and area, carry the rest. Without compression, the residual value depended more on the technical inputs: the total-order index of error reduction rose to \NumSobolCycZeroSTErrRed, behind discount rate (\NumSobolCycZeroSTDisc) and price (\NumSobolCycZeroSTPrice). At the baseline state and no compression, raising error reduction from zero to \NumCfgErrRedMax\% raised the median NPV from \NumRvNPVErrZero to \NumRvNPVErrFifty million USD, whereas one year of compression alone gave \NumRvNPVCycOneErrZero million USD.

Compression led under all five importance measures (Figure~\ref{fig:fig5_what_to_measure}b). Compression had the largest Shapley effect with correlated inputs (\NumShapleyCyc), ahead of discount rate (\NumShapleyDisc), price (\NumShapleyPrice) and adoption ceiling (\NumShapleyAdopt), and the largest PAWN index (\NumPawnCyc) \citep{Iooss2019Shapley,Puy2020Sensitivity}. The four minor technical inputs lay below every economic input on every measure. The leading active-subspace direction carried \NumASshareOne\% of the mean squared gradient and loaded mainly on compression (\NumASloadCycOne); the leading two directions carried \NumAScumTwo\%.

Resolving cycle compression is worth more than resolving any other input, about \NumEVPPIratioCycErr times as much as resolving error reduction (Figure~\ref{fig:fig5_what_to_measure}c). At the indifference hurdle, where the alternative pays the expected NPV of \NumEVprior million USD, the EVPPI was \NumEVPPIIndiffCyc million USD for compression, \NumEVPPIIndiffDisc for discount rate, \NumEVPPIIndiffPrice for price and \NumEVPPIIndiffAdopt for adoption ceiling, against \NumEVPPIIndiffErrRed for error reduction and \NumEVPPIIndiffCostRed for cost reduction \citep{Claxton2001Bayesian}. At a hurdle of zero, EVPPI was \NumEVPPIzeroHurdleMax for every input, because expected NPV is positive at every level of every input: without running costs, no pilot result would change the decision to invest. With a running cost of \NumCfgRunCostHigh million USD per year, the EVPPI of compression rose to \NumRvEVPPIzeroCycCostHigh million USD, against \NumRvEVPPIzeroErrCostHigh for error reduction (Table~\ref{tab:netvalue}). Discount rate and price describe the decision context rather than the tool, so a pilot should measure how far a tool shortens the cycle, then its error reduction.

\subsection{Robustness of the attribution}\label{sec:res_robust}

The economic context ranks as high as compression once the assumptions favoring compression are relaxed (Table~\ref{tab:robust}). Compression had the largest total-order index in \NumRvNcycFirst of the \NumRvNscenarios scenarios, and the discount rate in the other \NumRvNdiscFirst. The discount rate led when compression was limited to zero or one year (\NumRvSTDiscPriorZeroOne against \NumRvSTCycPriorZeroOne), when probability \NumCfgZeroMassB was put on no compression, under half-year steps, with a \NumCfgHorizonLong-year horizon (\NumRvSTDiscHorizonForty against \NumRvSTCycHorizonForty) and in both conservative combinations. Across scenarios, the index of compression ranged from \NumRvSTCycMin to \NumRvSTCycMax and that of error reduction from \NumRvSTErrMin to \NumRvSTErrMax.

The structural variants changed the level of value more than its attribution. Recurrent cycles raised expected NPV to \NumRvEVRecurrent million USD and a \NumCfgHorizonLong-year horizon to \NumRvEVHorizonForty million USD, against \NumRvEVBase million USD in the main analysis, whereas removing the adoption ramp left it at \NumRvEVRampStep million USD. Error reduction overtook compression among the technical inputs in one scenario. With no adoption ramp, a slow adoption rate (\NumCfgKLow), a \NumCfgHorizonLong-year horizon and compression of zero or one year, the total-order index of error reduction was \NumRvSTErrConservativeZeroOne and that of compression \NumRvSTCycConservativeZeroOne, and their EVPPI values were close (\NumRvEVPPIErrConservativeZeroOne and \NumRvEVPPICycConservativeZeroOne million USD). The calibration target scaled expected NPV (\NumRvEVTargetQuarter to \NumRvEVTargetOne million USD) but left the indices unchanged, and running costs raised the index of compression to as much as \NumRvSTCycCostHigh. Putting probability \NumCfgZeroMassA on no compression and spreading the rest over one to three years raised it to \NumRvSTCycPriorZeroMass, consistent with the slightly wider spread of compression outcomes under this prior. In every scenario, the EVPPI of compression exceeded that of error reduction, so the order of what a pilot should measure did not change even where the variance ranking did.

A tool noisier than manual scoring loses value unless it also shortens the cycle. In the stress block (error reduction \NumCfgStressErr\%, \NumNcellsStress profiles), per-cycle gain changed by \NumStressUpliftPct\% on average, between \NumStressDiffMin\% and \NumStressDiffMax\% across profiles, as cheaper plots offset part of the added noise. Without compression, the median NPV was \NumStressNPVCycZero million USD and positive in \NumStressPrPosCycZero\% of states. With one year of compression, it was \NumStressNPVCycOne million USD and positive in \NumStressPrPosCycOne\% of states. The main grid, which explores only nonnegative error reductions, therefore understates the downside of a tool whose measurements are noisier than manual scores, and the assumed time saving outweighs the added noise in this model (Supplementary Section S6).
